Xavier Barber · i-MInD / UMH

Research and knowledge transfer

Artificial Intelligence methods grounded in statistics and connected to real problems.

I am a Full Professor of Statistics and Operations Research at Universidad Miguel Hernández de Elche and a researcher at i-MInD — Instituto en Matemáticas, Inteligencia Artificial y Ciencia de Datos.

Artificial Intelligence: research and knowledge transfer

Research

I work on machine learning, Bayesian networks and interpretable models for prediction and decision support. Applications include species distribution, clinical research, and the estimation of production frontiers. Recent work also examines generative AI in clinical guideline interpretation.

Explore Artificial Intelligence and decision-making publications.

Knowledge transfer

  • Applied modelling and consulting: statistical and machine-learning support for clinical studies, environmental analysis, industry and public institutions.
  • Open-source software: co-development of the eat R package for efficiency analysis using tree-based methods.
  • Public-sector training: specialised courses in generative AI, prompt engineering, document analysis with large language models, and responsible use of AI in administrative workflows.
  • Health collaboration: leadership of the UMH–FISABIO Joint Research Unit on Statistical Methods in Health Sciences (STATSALUT).

Bayesian and spatio-temporal statistics

My interests include hierarchical spatial models, spatial misalignment, multivariate geostatistics and coregionalisation, species distribution modelling, and environmental forecasting. A central question is how to account for uncertainty when combining observations collected at different spatial and temporal scales.

Selected spatial and spatio-temporal papers.

Efficiency and productivity

With Juan Aparicio and colleagues, I combine production-frontier and Data Envelopment Analysis methods with machine learning. Our work includes efficiency analysis trees, regression splines, Random Forest and models with multiple outputs, alongside implementation in R.

Selected efficiency and productivity papers.
